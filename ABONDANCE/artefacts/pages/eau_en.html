Line of reasoning

Thales, and water as first principle

Thales' student Anaximander made water the principle of all things: wrong, but the first attempt to explain the world without a myth.

Archimedes, Pascal, Bernoulli: pressure becomes a number

Buoyancy, then von Guericke's barometer, then flow: hydrostatics and hydrodynamics become measurable before industry does.

van 't Hoff: osmotic pressure before the membranes

π = i·c·R·T dates from 1886. It sets the floor that reverse osmosis would take a century to approach.

Second law: the floor under desalination

Pulling fresh water out of the sea costs at least the reversed work of mixing: 0.8136 kWh/m³. Any promise below it is a bookkeeping error.

Close the cycle, then count the plate

The land cycle must leave nothing out: the columns close by themselves. Then virtual water puts the tap inside agriculture.

Sources and predecessors

This dossier starts from constants, not from a table of numbers: water receives ρgH from the electricity dossier and returns the pumping kWh/m³ that agriculture will spend. Predecessors named: Thales (water as first principle), Archimedes (buoyancy), Pascal (transmitted pressure), Bernoulli (energy of motion), Darcy (flow through rock), van 't Hoff and Pfeffer (osmotic pressure and osmometry), Gibbs (mixing work), Loeb and Sourirajan (asymmetric membrane for desalination); their performances stay declared figures, not measured here. Darcy, Pfeffer, Loeb and Sourirajan carry no equation in this dossier: lineage-only names, as SOURCES_ET_PREDECESSEURS.md at the root spells out.
