Line of reasoning

The measured source, and what it does not give

The starting point is a YouTube page measured byte for byte, cited as a bibliographic reference: “What Is Actually Hiding in the Digits of Pi?” (Euclidea channel, id M90ZAVojI30, declared length 854 s). It serves ten dated chapters, but timedtext returns 0 bytes in json3, srv3 and xml: the transcript is held to be absent. The dossier takes no quotation, no number and no method from it; it keeps ten themes, each attached to one equation.

The domain is closed before the read

n = 1000000 digits per constant, KMAX = 6 classes, RMAX = 7 run lengths, ALPHA = 0.001, GRAINE = 20261109, PAS_CUMUL = 10, five search probes: every one of these is written in the module before a single decimal is produced. The 108 cells of the confrontation are enumerated by `predire(n)`, which reads no digit at all.

The decimals come from a certificate, not from rounding

Each main path computes an exact enclosure at both bounds and checks that the two bounds share the same integer part at the requested scale: the digits are forced. The module even records one real correction — the anchor constant for √2 had been copied wrong, diverging at the 38th digit, and nothing confronted it; C01 does now.

Two independent paths are crossed

Chudnovsky against Machin for π, the factorial series against the term-by-term sum for e, the integer square root against Newton for √2. Agreement digit for digit testifies that the generator does not drift; it adds nothing new about the constants themselves.

The confrontation bounds rejection, it does not prove

108 cells, a two-sided threshold of 0.001 declared in advance, a deviance pooled over 18 degrees of freedom and a global Poisson verdict on the number of exclusions. One cell is excluded out of 108 where 0.108 were expected, with p = 0.2047: CONTENU. This does not say π is normal; on this domain it says nothing forces us to treat it as anything but a uniform sequence.

The bound is written in the module

`EQ-17` carries no word “proof”: the status of this whole dossier is a confrontation over a finite domain. Simple normality of π, e and √2 has remained open since Borel 1909; not one computed line here steps outside the declared domain, and the last chapter of the video, “A pattern nobody has proven”, is taken at its word.

The page serves no transcript (timedtext, 0 bytes): no sentence of the video is quoted, no number comes from it, and the title “Five digits at position 16470” is not the source of the figure 16470, which C09 recomputes from the decimals produced here. Both generating paths come from the literature and are declared as such: Chudnovsky and Chudnovsky (1989) for π = 426880·√10005 / Σ(−1)ᵏ(6k)!(A+Bk)/((3k)!(k!)³C³), Machin (1706) for the second path of π, Euler (1748) for e = Σ 1/k!, and the integer inequality w² ≤ 2·10²ⁿ < (w+1)² for √2. The statistical tools are public too: Pearson for χ², Wilson and Hilferty (1931) for the cube-root interval, Bonferroni (1936) and Cetoni (1925) for the multiplicity correction, Poisson for small counts, Fisher (1932) for the G test, Metropolis, Ulam and von Neumann (1949) for Monte-Carlo. Pure lineage names, on which no equation rests: Borel (1909), Sierpiński (1917), Champernowne (1933), Copeland and Erdős (1937), Wall (1949), Bailey, Borwein and Plouffe (1995) — neither of our two paths uses the BBP formula. What is computed here, and only here: the integer certificates, the 108 cells closed before the read, the three counting paths, the pooled deviance and the bounded verdict.
